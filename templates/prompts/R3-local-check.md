# R3-LOCAL — Examiner-Report Sweep Agent (Project Hero writer slice)

The local (default) variant of the R3 stage. The NLM variant was discovery-only — every claim needed local verification anyway, and its mistranscriptions (wrong MCQ answers, invented marking points, inverted facts) created a whole defect class. This stage makes extraction and verification the same act: **everything you emit is a verbatim quote with a file/line ref, by construction.**

You will be given parameters: SUBTOPIC, UNIT, SP_NAMES, CORPUS_DIR, RESEARCH_DIR, plus optional ESCALATION_NOTE (see end).

Inputs to read first: `<RESEARCH_DIR>/vault-digest.md` (spec text and scope per SP; legacy appearance tables only on adopted-vault courses) and `<RESEARCH_DIR>/ms-extracts.md`, whose top section is R2's per-sitting appearance record (**must already exist — if it is missing or clearly incomplete, STOP and report; do not proceed**, your coverage diff in section 4 is meaningless without it).

Corpus: `<CORPUS_DIR>`. Resit/variant sittings (e.g. "(A)"-suffixed) are SEPARATE files. Some sittings have no ER file at all — record which, don't infer.

## Task — systematic per-sitting ER sweep

For EVERY sitting in the unit's corpus that has an Examiner report file:

1. **Locate this subtopic's questions** in that ER using the appearance table's refs. **The whitespace-tolerant header regex is the ONLY permitted method (2026-08-10 — never enumerate literal forms):** search `Q\s*<n>\s*\(?` and `Question\s*<n>` as regexes, plus a distinctive stem content word. "Question 4 (a)" with a space before the bracket beat the old enumerated-forms rule and produced a false "no ER comment" after that rule shipped. Only record "no ER comment" when regex AND content-word sweeps both miss.
2. **Also sweep by content**: grep the ER for the SP's load-bearing terms (equation names, key terms, apparatus/sources/set-texts — whatever the subject's equivalents are) **and the SP's equation-SYMBOL forms** (`W\s*=\s*mg` — a question whose ER never says "weight" is invisible to every word sweep, 2026-08-10). ERs sometimes discuss an error class in a general section without a question header. Filename matching throughout is case-insensitive ("Mark Scheme"/"Mark scheme" coexist in one corpus).
3. **Extract verbatim** every relevant passage, with sitting attribution and the ER file's line number. Quotes running to a `## Page N` marker MUST be continued past it. Never paraphrase inside quote marks; your own words only in `[note: …]` labels.
4. **Characterisation gate:** when you describe what a question IS about (its apparatus/set-up/source material, the quantity or analysis asked for), open the QP and confirm — sitting and topic agreement is not enough.

## Output — `<RESEARCH_DIR>/nlm-check.md` (same filename and section skeleton as the NLM variant, so downstream prompts need no change)

1. **Verified insights** — cross-sitting patterns you can evidence with ≥1 verbatim ER quote each (recurring error classes, marking-emphasis themes). Every claim carries its quotes and refs. A pattern claimed across N sittings must quote or ref all N.
   **Sitting-count semantics (2026-08-10 — most of a wave's count corrections were exactly these two conflations):** a sitting count means *distinct sittings with ER-narrated evidence*. An MS-note-only leg (a marking-point or guidance line with no ER narration) is listed separately, never folded into the sitting count; and an MS quote from a sitting already counted is not a further sitting. Say which legs are which.
   **Roll-up claims are written FROM their list, after the list is final (F34, ported from R2 — every failed superlative in the 4PH1 wave originated in an R3 §1):** any summary sentence, heading or tally over your extracted passages ("appears in almost every sitting", "the most common error") must be derived from the finished quote list and re-read against it — a roll-up written from memory of the sweep is the defect class that survives every row-by-row re-check. Six of nineteen is not "almost every".
2. **Already in R1/R2 (duplicate)** — insights the digest/extracts already carry; list refs only.
3. **Unverified** — should be EMPTY by construction. If you were tempted to state something you could not quote, name it here as "not evidenced locally" instead of stating it.
4. **Refs not covered by R2 (mark-scheme coverage gate — same as the NLM variant)** — every question ref your section-1 insights introduce that `ms-extracts.md` does not cover, each with sitting, tariff and marking points read directly from the MS (quote the MS lines with line refs). The orchestrator tops up R2 from this list before the writer runs. **Every ref carries an explicit disposition — `GAP — needs extraction` or `CONFIRMED — sibling-owned, no action` — and when every ref is confirmatory the section is headed `**None.**` (F105: a §4 that files confirmations under the heading for gaps makes the section unusable as a work-list, and the orchestrator triaging by heading finds nothing to do in exactly the files where the real defects are. §4 must be readable as a queue).** **§4b — NEW MATERIAL ON REFS R2 ALREADY COVERED (F162, mandatory even when §4 reads None):** §4's test is a REF test, and a ref test passes over content — a ResultsPlus exemplar pair (candidate answers with examiner comments) and a boxed Examiner Tip on an already-extracted ref once stayed in this file, treated downstream as context rather than evidence, while every automated gate reported clean; a human reading prose caught it. That class is the highest-value ET&T material the corpus holds. So: for every ref you touched, where the ER or MS carries material `ms-extracts.md` does NOT — exemplar answers with comments, boxed tips, guidance beyond what R2 quoted — list it here (sitting, ref, one-line description, file:line) as a top-up queue exactly like §4's. When there is none, head the sub-section **None.** — the heading exists either way, so its absence is detectable.
5. **Summary** — sittings swept / ERs absent / passages extracted; the 2–3 most valuable insights.

## Hard rules

- Every number/count claim: per-file `grep -c` only, never `files_with_matches` totals
- Known corpus casualties: check `project.json` → `corpus.known_casualties` and the unit's wave-state file for the current list — never cite an item on it (file path or notebook source title)
- **Content limitations (F65):** check `project.json` → `corpus.content_limitations` — content *classes* unreliable in every file. Each entry's `mandatory_action` binds you; above all, never assert an absence inside a limited class from the converted markdown alone. **If the key is MISSING rather than empty, that is a setup defect — report it; never read absence-of-the-key as "no limitations" (F100: an agent did exactly that, correctly, on a course with three live limitation classes — a missing key and a clean corpus must not be the same observation)**
- **Anomalies go to the primary source first, by THREE routes (F62, amended per F107/F109):** before recording an absence claim, a cross-source contradiction, or an ER/MS row you cannot match to a stem — (1) the second-engine markdown tree where the project has one, (2) the PDF text layer, (3) the RENDERED page image (symbol-font characters are drawn, not typed — a text read of the PDF is not always enough; and per W-176 a RENDER can itself be corrupt via font substitution — where it is, triangulate the reading from a second document rather than the corrupt glyph). The converted file is working evidence, not the paper. What you cannot resolve goes in your return as `unresolved_anomalies`, never silently smoothed over
- **Existence claims are recursive and print their command (F106 — BLOCKER class, W-49):** any claim that a file, directory, tool or corpus resource does not exist must be established with a RECURSIVE check (`find <root> -iname "*.pdf"` style), and the claim must quote the command and its output. A check at one directory level is a check on a folder, not on a corpus — "no PDFs exist under research/corpus/" was written while 61 PDFs sat one level down, and it disabled the pack's court of last resort for a whole wave (the six false damage claims of that wave were unfalsifiable only because of it). **A restatement is not a check:** repeating an upstream claim you did not test yourself is written as "per §X, untested here" — never "§X's confirmation" (a claim gained authority in transit exactly that way). And per the standing rule: a claim that survives a context compaction has been repeated, not checked
- ESCALATION_NOTE: if the orchestrator has authorised NLM escalation and your sweep for a subtopic comes back genuinely thin (fewer than ~3 usable ER passages across the corpus), say so explicitly in the Summary and recommend an NLM escalation query — do NOT run one yourself unless the parameters include a NOTEBOOK_ID and say to

Return: counts per output section + the 2–3 most valuable verified insights + any data-quality flags + `unresolved_anomalies` (state "none" explicitly).
